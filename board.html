Building a Culture of Peace - Ten Critical Seconds

Melrose Primary and Infant School is more than a place where children learn Mathematics, Language Arts, and Science. It is also a community in which they learn how to respond to disappointment, manage anger, resolve conflict and live respectfully with others.

Anger and interpersonal conflict remain constant challenges, not only at Melrose, but throughout the wider community. Children will experience provocation, disagreement and frustration. Our responsibility is not to pretend that these situations will disappear, but to equip them with practical ways of responding.

During a recent school devotion, the children were introduced to a simple traffic-light approach:

Red - Stop.

Do not immediately hit, push, insult or retaliate. Stop before the first angry impulse determines what happens next.

Yellow - Calm down and think.

Take a few deep breaths. Count slowly to ten. Say a brief prayer such as, “God, help me to do what is right.”

Then ask:

  • What exactly happened?
  • What might happen if I react angrily?
  • What is the safest and wisest thing to do next?

These simple actions give the body time to settle so that the mind can make better decisions.

Green - Choose and act.

Speak calmly, walk away, seek help or involve a teacher or another trusted adult.

This approach can help children create a critical pause between what happens to them and what they choose to do next. Within that pause, a fight can be prevented, a hurtful word can remain unspoken, and a better decision can be made.

However, children cannot be expected to develop self-control in isolation. They learn from what they see around them. Parents, educators and community members must therefore model the behaviour we want them to adopt. When adults pause before reacting, disagree without becoming disrespectful and resolve conflict without aggression, we demonstrate that strength is not measured by how forcefully we respond, but by how wisely we choose.

The biblical instruction remains profoundly practical:

“Everyone should be quick to listen, slow to speak and slow to become angry.”
— James 1:19, NIV

As Community Representative, I encourage us to reinforce one consistent message at school, at home and throughout the community:

We may not control what happens to us, but we can control how we respond.

If we can help our children to stop, calm themselves, think through the consequences and choose wisely, we will be doing more than reducing conflict at school. We will be helping to develop thoughtful, responsible and peaceful citizens.
